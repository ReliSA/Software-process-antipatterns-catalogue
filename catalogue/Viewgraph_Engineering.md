# Viewgraph Engineering

## Also Known As

n/a

## Summary

Viewgraph Engineering is an anti-pattern in which developers spend a disproportionate amount of time producing presentations, diagrams, and documentation instead of building working software. Organizational pressure for formal reporting and visibility pushes skilled developers away from implementation work, reducing overall productivity and increasing frustration within the team.

## Context

This anti-pattern can appear in any software development methodology where organizational or managerial constraints require developers to regularly produce formal reports, architectural diagrams, presentations, or other non-code artifacts.

## Unbalanced Forces

- Management requires formal documentation and presentations to track and communicate project progress.
- Developers' core expertise and value lies in building working software, not in producing office documents.
- Organizations lack dedicated technical writers, business analysts, or other roles suited to documentation work, so the burden falls on developers.
- Formal artifacts create the appearance of progress even when little working software has been produced.

## Symptoms and Consequences

- Developers spend more time producing documents, slides, and diagrams than writing code.
- High frustration and declining motivation in development teams.
- Delivery of working software is delayed.

## Causes

- Organizational focus on reporting and documentation over working software.
- Management's lack of understanding of technical work.
- Developers' attempt to comply with reporting requirements instead of focusing on implementation.

## (Refactored) Solution

- Instead of investing significant time in creating slides, diagrams, and detailed documents, developers should build lightweight prototypes and mockups early in the process.
- Shift the primary measure of project progress from documentation to working software.
- Documentation should support development, not replace it.
- Limit the scope and formality of required documents to what is genuinely necessary for decision-making and communication.
